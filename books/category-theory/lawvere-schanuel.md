# Lawvere–Schanuel — *Conceptual Mathematics*

**F. William Lawvere and Stephen H. Schanuel**,
*Conceptual Mathematics: A First Introduction to Categories*,
2nd ed., Cambridge University Press, 2009.
[Publisher and contents](https://www.cambridge.org/highereducation/books/conceptual-mathematics/00772F4CC3D4268200C5EC86B39D415A).
The reader specifically liked this book as a first introduction. Ownership
and edition of a physical copy have not been checked.

These are **original working mathematical notes**, aligned to the book's
published contents. They are not quotations, an author's theory of couples,
or a claim of complete page-by-page study.

## I. Sets, maps and composition

For a category 𝒞, a map f : A → B has specified domain A and codomain B.
If f : A → B and g : B → C, their composite g ∘ f : A → C is defined.
For h : C → D,

    h ∘ (g ∘ f) = (h ∘ g) ∘ f.

Every object A has identity 1_A, satisfying f ∘ 1_A = f and
1_B ∘ f = f. These are actual axioms, not metaphors.

It is tempting to put the two people A and B at the endpoints of arrows
and call an interaction f : A → B. That can be useful in a *free category
on a directed interaction graph*: arrow labels might be "invites" and
"answers"; composites are directed paths. If A → B → A is a conversation
cycle, however, the composite A → A need not be identity. The participants
can return to an earlier topic without returning to their earlier
relationship state.

A separate category can have **relationship states** r₀, r₁, ... as
objects and dated episodes/changes as generating arrows. Do not confuse
this category with one whose objects are the people.

**Test:** specify what two directed episodes mean when composed.
Do we retain the intervening episode, or are we collapsing it into a
single unverified causal summary? If it is the latter, the purported
composition has thrown away precisely what Yente needs to remember.

## II. Isomorphisms, opposed maps, sections, retractions

An isomorphism f : A → B has inverse g : B → A such that

    g ∘ f = 1_A       and       f ∘ g = 1_B.

A mutual exchange of expressions of love supplies neither equation.
Likewise, symmetry of reported affection is not mathematical equivalence.
These distinctions make the word *isomorphism* more useful, not less.

For f : A → B, a **section** s : B → A with f ∘ s = 1_B
makes f a split epimorphism; a **retraction** r : B → A with
r ∘ f = 1_A makes f a split monomorphism. (Naming varies with
which arrow is described; write the equations before using the names.)

An idempotent e : A → A satisfies e² = e. In Set this can represent
a normalization that sends every input to a preferred representative
and then stops changing it. If Yente repeatedly organizes remembered
evidence into an indexed summary, an idempotent *normalization*
may be appropriate. A summary of A's account **cannot** be treated
as an idempotent normalizer of B's motives.

An **involution** f : A → A satisfies f² = 1_A. A time-indexed,
symmetric, exclusive matching of people can be represented by a
*fixed-point-free involution* only on those people who are paired.
On the full population, unmatched people require a partial involution
(or fixed points with an explicit different meaning). This models
matching combinatorics, not the content of a relationship.

**Counterexample:** A → B is "A remembers B's remark"; B → A is
"B appreciates that". Neither arrow reverses the other by composition.
Calling them "inverse" would erase rather than describe the encounter.

## III. Structured sets, graphs and diagrams

A graph may admit many arrows between the same two vertices:
different interactions, times, speakers and directions. A category
freely generated by a graph adds paths and empty paths (identities).
This preserves alternative histories with equal start and end points.

A *structured set* carries more than its underlying elements.
Homomorphisms/morphisms in its category must preserve the designated
structure. Thus before claiming R₀ ≅ R₁ we must say which aspects of
a relationship count as structure. Shared calendar? Reciprocity as
reported by whom? Rules of cohabitation? Reported trust? Different
answers produce different categories.

A diagram is *commutative* if specified parallel composites are equal.
People can supply incompatible accounts of an incident. That is not a
defect to erase by declaring their diagram commutative; the disagreement
is part of the data. For Yente, store each attributed path, test
coherence later, and allow a path to be unknown or contested.

## IV. Universal mapping properties

**Terminal object.** An object 1 is terminal if for every X exactly
one morphism X → 1 exists. In Set, every singleton is terminal.
This is a mathematical property of a whole category, not "the one"
ideal partner.

**Product.** A × B has projections π_A : A × B → A and
π_B : A × B → B. For any X with f : X → A, g : X → B, exactly one
⟨f,g⟩ : X → A × B satisfies

    π_A ∘ ⟨f,g⟩ = f;     π_B ∘ ⟨f,g⟩ = g.

Example: if A and B are sets of possible reports, a paired record
is a point of A × B. But the product includes **every possible pair**
of reports and does not tell us which are historically related.
A particular encounter may be a relation E ⊆ A × B, with additional
context and provenance. Nor does taking the product generate the shared
relationship as an emergent social reality.

**Coproduct.** In Set, A ⊔ B is a disjoint union, keeping origin
tags. This is a useful first image for retaining two speakers'
reports without silently conflating them. The coproduct's universal
property specifies maps *out*, rather than the product's maps *in*.

**Pullback.** For f : A → S and g : B → S, the Set pullback is

    A ×_S B = { (a,b) ∈ A × B | f(a) = g(b) }.

Concrete synthetic example: A and B are lists of available appointment
options, and f,g extract the meeting slot. The pullback lists feasible
pairs with a common slot. This *is* a checkable compatibility operation.
It does not measure affection; compatibility is relative to S and to
what f,g capture. Change S from "time" to "time and location", and the
result changes.

A universal property characterizes the desired construction by
behavior among arrows, usually up to unique isomorphism.
"Universal" does **not** mean "universally valid for couples."

## V. Map objects, contravariance, components

Where exponentials exist, B^A represents maps A → B; the evaluation
map ev : B^A × A → B applies a map to an argument. For Set, B^A is
the set of functions. This is a language for models or transformations,
not a claim that every emotional possibility is already a total function.

Taking preimages under f : A → B gives a function

    f⁻¹ : ℘(B) → ℘(A)

and reverses arrow direction. The same observation can be described
under one person's public vocabulary and projected to the permitted
evidence supporting it, but the construction alone does not establish
what was *actually known* or what one person consented to reveal.
If disclosure is part of a typed system, it needs independent
authorization checks.

Connected components in an undirected graph group vertices linked
by paths. In a social graph, components describe *network connection*,
not emotional closeness or trust. Directed reachability differs from
undirected connectedness; do not exchange them casually.

## VI. Adjoints and discipline

The book's appendix introduces adjoints with graph/dynamical-system
examples. For functors F : 𝒞 → 𝒟 and G : 𝒟 → 𝒞,
an adjunction F ⊣ G consists of natural bijections

    Hom_𝒟(FX,Y) ≅ Hom_𝒞(X,GY).

It is not merely a harmonious arrangement of complementary roles.
One must provide categories, functors, and natural hom-set bijections.
A couple is not an adjunction merely because people cooperate.

## Exercises for this repo

1. Construct the free category of a tiny *synthetic* exchange:
   invitation A → B, reply B → A, later revised invitation A → B.
   Count the two distinct parallel paths; explain why neither
   composite is an identity without new equations.
2. Give a relation of story fragments E ⊆ A × B that is not equal
   to the whole product. What extra information selects E?
3. Make two pullbacks of appointment options, first by time and
   then by time-and-place, to see a previously feasible pair vanish.
4. Exhibit a pair of opposing functions that are not inverses.
5. Specify a diagram that does **not** commute because speakers'
   interpretations differ. Keep the difference without "repairing"
   it by decree.
