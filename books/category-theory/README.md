# Category theory: reading guide and original notes

This is a mathematical research lane for Shallow/Yente. Its purpose is to
discover useful *descriptions of relations and compositions*, not to replace
the particular history of two people with a temperament label. The user
specifically distinguished **how the relationship evolves** from **how each
person changes because of the relationship**. Both matter, and neither
determines the other.

## Read in this order

1. F. William Lawvere and Stephen H. Schanuel, *Conceptual Mathematics:
   A First Introduction to Categories*, 2nd ed. (Cambridge, 2009).
   Begin with objects/maps, composition, opposed maps, sections/retractions,
   structured sets and graphs; continue to products and universal properties.
   **The user's liked first book.** [Notes](lawvere-schanuel.md);
   [publisher](https://www.cambridge.org/highereducation/books/conceptual-mathematics/00772F4CC3D4268200C5EC86B39D415A).
2. Steve Awodey, *Category Theory*, 2nd ed. (Oxford, 2010).
   Build the formal language: duality, diagrams, naturality, limits, colimits,
   exponential objects, adjoints, monads. [Notes](awodey.md);
   [2nd-edition bibliographic record](https://old.maa.org/press/maa-reviews/category-theory).
3. Tom Leinster, *Basic Category Theory* (Cambridge, 2014).
   Short and rigorous; compare its three approaches to universal properties:
   adjoints, representables, limits. The author's
   [site](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~tl/bct/) links to an authorized
   [open version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.09375).
4. Emily Riehl, *Category Theory in Context* (Dover, 2016).
   Useful for disciplined examples and wider context.
   [Author's site](https://math.jhu.edu/~eriehl/context/) permits free PDF
   access for personal use, not redistribution.
5. Brendan Fong and David I. Spivak, *Seven Sketches in Compositionality:
   An Invitation to Applied Category Theory* (Cambridge, 2019).
   For composed processes, multiple agents, databases, information flow and
   interfaces. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05316).
6. Saunders Mac Lane, *Categories for the Working Mathematician*, 2nd ed.
   (Springer, 1998): deeper reference, not a substitute for the above
   introductions. [Publisher](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-4721-8).

## Three distinct mathematical questions

**1. What is the relation at time t?** This could mean the ongoing shared
history, mutual expectations, arrangements, recorded interactions, or
interpretations of them. We must choose the relevant structure rather than
using the word "relationship" as though it specified a category automatically.

**2. How does it evolve?** Track R₀ → R₁ → R₂ as an evolving relational
object, with transition labels, competing histories, constraints, and changing
circumstances. This is not the same as asserting that A₀ → A₁ or B₀ → B₁
represents growth of either participant.

**3. Who sees which part?** A's experience of B, B's experience of A,
Yente's hypotheses, and what each authorizes Yente to reveal to C are
different sources/views. No categorical projection entails permission.

A useful analogy should identify its objects, arrows, composition law,
identity arrows, and any extra axioms. Otherwise it is **terminology**, not a
mathematical model.

## Research discipline

- Do not call mutual attraction an isomorphism. Inverse morphisms satisfy
  exact equations; affection and reciprocity generally do not.
- The cartesian product A × B does **not** itself represent a lived bond.
  It contains possible pairs. A particular relationship demands additional
  histories, actions, context and structure.
- A commutative diagram is a property to **test**, not a diagram to impose
  on an inconsistent pair of accounts.
- State transitions do not prove causation. Alternative paths must not be
  collapsed merely because they have common endpoints.
- Category theory cannot establish monogamy as desirable, predict a
  match, produce consent, or authorize sharing a third person's data.
- Keep [lived experience](../../lived-experience/README.md) and
  [corrections](../../lived-experience/correction-requirements.md) ahead
  of textbook analogies.
