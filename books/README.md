## Relationship literature to study

- Fyodor Dostoevsky — *The Idiot* (1868–1869), especially Part III, Chapter VIII. Aglaya tells Prince Myshkin that she wants to tell him everything, expects him to conceal nothing in return, and says, in Eva Martin's translation: “I want to speak to at least one person, as I would to myself.” The line is not a detachable maxim about romance. It occurs during her attempt to make Myshkin a confidant, ally, possible escape route, and person who will take her inner life seriously while her family interprets the scene as courtship. Useful for studying mutual disclosure, the wish to be intelligible without self-marketing, and the unstable boundary among friendship, conspiracy, dependence, and love. Text: https://americanliterature.com/author/fyodor-dostoevsky/book/the-idiot/part-iii-chapter-viii.
- Peter Handke — *Short Letter, Long Farewell* (German original, 1972; English translation by Ralph Manheim, 1974). The standard English title does not begin with “A.” An estranged husband crosses the United States while receiving messages from and pursuing, fleeing, or imagining pursuit by his wife. Useful not as a guide to healthy love but as a serious literary study of separation, obsession, narration, identity, and what remains of a bond after ordinary companionship has failed. Publisher: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/74639/short-letter-long-farewell-by-peter-handke/.

These works belong in the research corpus because Shallow should study what people have made of meeting, longing, disclosure, estrangement, marriage, and separation—not only manuals that flatten those experiences into techniques.

## Research notes: *Taking the War Out of Our Words*

- Author: Sharon Strand Ellison. 2009 edition: Wyatt-MacKenzie, 287 pages; 2016 edition: Voices of Integrity, 304 pages.
- Framework: Powerful Non-Defensive Communication (PNDC). Three defensive strategies: surrender, withdrawal, counterattack; six subpatterns: surrender-betray, surrender-sabotage, withdrawal-escape, withdrawal-entrap, counterattack-justify, counterattack-blame.
- Alternative tools: genuine questions, direct nonjudgmental statements, and predictions about one's own actions or boundaries rather than coercive threats.
- Relevance: divorce mediation, co-parenting, and financial conflict; possible source for questions about specific past interactions in Shallow. Treat these categories as a heuristic, not validated personality diagnoses.
- Table of contents and editions: https://openlibrary.org/books/OL27137203M/Taking_the_war_out_of_our_words.
- Publisher: https://www.pndc.com/products/.
- Mediation discussion: https://mediate.com/difficult-communications-going-beyond-i-statements/.
- Comparison with Rosenberg NVC: https://livingcompassion.com/taking-war-words/.
