# Correction requirements

These requirements were prompted by the conversation accompanying the user's request for learning from real life. They describe failures of the assistant and obligations of the proposed system. They do not publish personal case narratives or assert facts about absent people.

## Preserve the event before interpreting it

Retain who did what, to whom, in which setting, and in what sequence. Keep the magnitude and frequency actually supplied. Do not change the direction of an interaction or blend different people, settings, and periods into one biography.

Keep reported events, the speaker's interpretation, the assistant's hypotheses, and unknown information separate. Reports should not be dismissed merely because they are reports; hypotheses should not become witnessed facts merely because they sound plausible.

## Do not strengthen a statement without evidence

A request for less of something is not a request for its total prohibition. A complaint about an extreme pattern is not a demand for perfection. Not knowing one task does not imply wanting to be taught everything. A statement about necessary work does not establish a desire to command another person or a wish to obey them.

Those distinctions concern the meaning of the supplied account. They are not personality categories.

## Remove the consequences of a corrected assumption

After a correction, identify the earlier assumption and the claims that depended on it. Mark them as withdrawn or narrowed. Later replies must not revive them as facts about the user.

Replacing a rejected motive with a different invented motive is not an update. Acknowledgment followed by repetition is not learning. The same applies to repeating the user's correction as though it were new advice from the assistant.

## Respect the purpose for which information was offered

Background interests can calibrate cultural reference points without becoming explanations of a relationship. An analogy is not a causal account. An author's name is not evidence for a paragraph the assistant invented.

A request to stop advice must change the response, not merely its heading. Do not respond with another plan, script, checklist, or menu unless the user requests that form.

## Preserve lived history instead of replacing it with a type

Keep initial hopes, subsequent events, constraints, attempts at repair, changes, and outcomes together. An outcome does not automatically explain its own cause. Failure does not by itself establish blame, and later difficulty does not prove that earlier affinity was imaginary.

Do not infer temperament or fit from occupation, class, or taste. Do not assume resources, authority, or mobility that the account does not establish. A general framework may suggest a question; it cannot supply the missing event.

## Replay the assistant, not the people in the story

Future evaluation should use authorized cases and their corrections to inspect later assistant responses. It should ask whether the response retains the corrected distinction, answers the actual question, and separates evidence from uncertainty.

These are tests of system behavior. They are not deadlines, quotas, compatibility scores, or experiments to impose on a person. A document describing them does not establish that a running system already passes them.

## Publication boundary

No detailed personal case history is stored in this file. The attempted case-narrative write was not published. A private, user-authorized source store and review of any proposed public examples remain necessary before detailed cases can be retained here.
