# Premarital resource index

The source archive is split so that a generic communication workbook does not become the silent default against which every religious, legal, racial, queer, disabled, or interfaith couple is treated as a variation.

## What the archive is for

Use these documents to:

1. collect concrete questions couples are otherwise unlikely to ask;
2. compare different accounts of promise, permanence, family, sex, property, care, law, and legitimate exit;
3. identify legal, financial, medical, religious, and safety matters that require outside expertise;
4. expose assumptions hidden inside supposedly neutral curricula;
5. design Shallow's own transparent question and evidence system without copying a program's typology or marketing claims.

Do not use the archive to:

- produce a compatibility percentage;
- claim that one curriculum prevents divorce;
- treat every continuing marriage as a successful outcome;
- collapse abuse into poor communication;
- infer a person's beliefs from religious, racial, disability, gender, or sexual-identity labels;
- present old legal material as current advice;
- import John Gottman or Gottman-associated claims as Shallow's evidence base.

## Source classes

- **Core question source:** detailed prompts worth adapting after checking assumptions and provenance.
- **Comparative commitment source:** states what a tradition believes the marriage promise obligates.
- **Legal/economic source:** identifies institutional consequences that must be verified for current jurisdiction and date.
- **Safety source:** distinguishes conflict from coercion, violence, exploitation, or incapacity.
- **Research lead:** identifies a promising work that still requires the original text or stronger verification.
- **Topic map only:** useful for seeing what programs commonly discuss, but too dated, weakly supported, commercial, or derivative to ground Shallow.

## Remaining research lanes

- complete Sikh, Jain, Bahá'í, Zoroastrian, and secular-humanist preparation manuals;
- Deaf and DeafBlind materials in accessible visual and signed formats;
- disability-led neurodivergent premarital curricula;
- transnational marriage, immigration sponsorship, and cross-border parentage;
- military, maritime, trucking, warehouse, medical, and other shift-work relationships organized around absence and fatigue;
- labor-union, working-class, and low-income curricula that do not translate material deprivation into communication failure;
- Native American and other Indigenous community preparation, with tribal jurisdiction and kinship kept explicit;
- remarriage after divorce or widowhood beyond older-professional-class settings;
- preparation for couples who reject monogamy or legal marriage but make substantial commitments, without pretending those commitments are identical to civil marriage.
