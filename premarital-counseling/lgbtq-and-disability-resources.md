# LGBTQ and disability-centered marriage-preparation resources

These materials expose two failures of generic premarital counseling:

1. it often assumes a heterosexual couple whose parentage, kin recognition, religion, and public safety are unproblematic; and
2. it assumes two nondisabled people can marry without losing income, health care, personal assistance, autonomy, or legal capacity.

The point is not to create separate “special populations” modules after a supposedly neutral core. The supposedly neutral core already contains assumptions about bodies, law, family recognition, money, communication, care, and power.

## LGBTQ-inclusive preparation

### Unitarian Universalist *Premarital Counseling Guide for Same Gender Couples*

- Publisher: Unitarian Universalist Association, Office of Bisexual, Gay, Lesbian, and Transgender Concerns.
- Link: https://www.uua.org/files/documents/lgbtq/pre-maritalcounseling.pdf.
- Structure: four-session clergy guide with nine reproducible handouts.
- Coverage: family histories, goals, religion, gender roles, success, money, property, household labor, relatives and prior relationships, sex, affection, anger, children, health, durable powers of attorney, and the couple's implicit contract.
- Useful feature: asks what each partner assumes without having negotiated and treats institutional support before and after the ceremony as part of recognizing the relationship.
- Caution: older material. Its legal references, terminology, bibliography, and optional personality instruments are dated; do not import the suggested Myers-Briggs material. Keep the concrete questions and institutional insight while verifying every legal claim.
- Classification: **core question source with historical and psychometric screening**.

### Episcopal Church pastoral resources for gender and sexual minority couples

- Publisher: The Episcopal Church, Standing Commission on Liturgy and Music.
- Full resource: https://www.episcopalchurch.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/lm_i_will_bless_you_and_you_will_be_a_blessing-marriage_liturgy.pdf.
- Later marriage-preparation resource for any couple: https://www.episcopalchurch.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/lm_liturgical_resources_2_full_text.pdf.
- Coverage: a five-session process; covenant and lifelong intention; family histories; acceptance or rejection by relatives; culture; money; sex and monogamy; alcohol and drugs; children; legal protections; household roles; decision-making; support networks; and liturgical commitments.
- Useful feature: began from preparation for same-sex couples and was later adapted for any couple, reversing the usual pattern in which heterosexual assumptions are merely edited at the margins.
- Caution: an Episcopal theological and pastoral source, not a universal psychological model. Some legal advice reflects the period in which the original material was written.
- Classification: **comparative commitment and practical question source**.

### Nancy H. Wiener, “Pre-marital Counseling for Same-sex Couples: Highlights for Rabbis and Cantors”

- Host: The Shalom Center archive.
- Link: https://legacy4now.theshalomcenter.org/node/316.
- Coverage: relationship history, family and community recognition, support and rejection, Jewish ritual, legal documents, public disclosure, and creation of a new family status.
- Useful feature: shows that “family support” is not a generic asset. Partners may have unequal safety, disclosure, and acceptance in their own families, workplaces, and religious communities.
- Caution: published in 2001; legal specifics are obsolete. Preserve the pastoral questions, not the old legal environment.
- Classification: **historical pastoral question source**.

### SAGE, *Talk Before You Walk*

- Publisher: SAGE / National Resource Center on LGBTQ+ Aging.
- Link: https://lgbtagingcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2025/08/talk-before-you-walk-sage-finance-toolkit-2016.pdf.
- Focus: older LGBTQ couples considering whether civil marriage helps or harms their financial, health-care, estate, retirement, housing, and benefit arrangements.
- Useful feature: starts from “whether to marry” rather than presuming that legal marriage is always the right expression of commitment.
- Caution: originally produced after *Obergefell* and contains legal and benefit statements that may be outdated. Use it to identify questions, then verify current federal and state law.
- Classification: **legal/economic topic map for older couples**.

## Parentage and family-formation questions

Civil marriage does not by itself settle every issue for LGBTQ families. Preparation should separately examine:

- legal parentage for each intended parent;
- donor agreements and clinic records;
- adoption or confirmatory adoption where advisable;
- surrogacy law and independent counsel;
- disposition of embryos, eggs, sperm, and reproductive tissue after separation, incapacity, or death;
- recognition across state or national borders;
- school, medical, and emergency authority;
- names, identity documents, immigration, and citizenship;
- obligations to known donors, former partners, and existing children;
- what kinship terms and roles the household will use.

These are legal questions requiring current jurisdiction-specific advice, not matters a compatibility questionnaire can settle.

## Disability, chronic illness, and marriage

### *Our Sexuality, Our Health: A Disabled Advocate's Guide to Relationships, Romance, Sexuality and Sexual Health*

- Publisher: Office of Developmental Primary Care at the University of California, San Francisco/UC Berkeley-associated disability-health work; hosted by disability organizations.
- Link: https://www.williams-syndrome.org/files/inline-files/2018_08_16_dissex_guide_final_proof.pdf.
- Coverage: accessible sexuality education, power, touch, isolation, doctors, contraception, violence, dating, moving in, sex, consent, marriage, pregnancy, and parenting.
- Useful feature: written in a disabled first-person plural rather than treating disabled adults as objects of family or professional management. It directly addresses the marriage penalty, personal assistance, household labor, and the difference between support and control.
- Classification: **core disability-rights and relationship source**.

### Disability Rights Education & Defense Fund, *Marriage Equality Toolkit*

- Publisher: Disability Rights Education & Defense Fund.
- Link: https://dredf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/DREDF-Marriage-Equality-Toolkit_March-2026.pdf.
- Coverage: Supplemental Security Income, Disabled Adult Child benefits, Medicaid, long-term services and supports, spousal deeming, asset limits, the “holding out” rule, and current reform efforts.
- Useful feature: makes clear that legal marriage can threaten the income and services that permit a disabled person to live independently. For some couples, refusing civil marriage may protect the relationship materially rather than indicate weaker commitment.
- Caution: benefits rules and pending legislation can change. Verify current Social Security, Medicaid, tax, and state-waiver rules before action.
- Classification: **current legal/economic source**.

### *My Marriage My Choice* toolkit

- Producers: University of Nottingham and University of Kent researchers with the Ann Craft Trust and Respond; funded by the UK National Institute for Health Research School for Social Care Research.
- Link: https://sscr.nihr.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/86.-My-Marriage-My-Choice-Toolkit.pdf.
- Focus: consent to marriage, learning disability, forced marriage, decision-specific capacity, supported learning, safeguarding, communication, and professional assessment.
- Useful feature: distinguishes incapacity, lack of information, coercion, family pressure, and a person's own wish. It also stresses that support should maximize the person's ability to decide rather than using disability as a shortcut to prohibition.
- Caution: UK law and professional practice; it is not a U.S. capacity guide. Its strongest transferable contribution is the separation of support, assessment, consent, and coercion.
- Classification: **capacity, consent, and safeguarding source**.

### NCFR, *A Couple's Therapy Framework for Adult Couples with an Intellectual Disability*

- Authors: Rebecca R. Kammes and Megan Lachmar.
- Host: National Council on Family Relations.
- Link: https://www.ncfr.org/sites/default/files/2017-12/405-81%20FT%20-%20A%20Couples%20Therapy%20Framework%20for%20Adult%20Couples%20with%20an%20Intellectual%20Disability.pdf.
- Coverage: expectations, romance and sexual intimacy, family approval, support, decision-making, gender narratives, differences, families, service providers, and the couple's own goals.
- Useful feature: includes service providers and family systems as possible influences on the relationship rather than assuming the couple controls all conditions around it.
- Caution: brief professional framework, not a complete premarital curriculum or validation study.
- Classification: **accessible assessment and systems source**.

## Neurodivergent couples: design requirements

The search found many private-practice pages and generic AI-like guides, but no strong, complete, open premarital curriculum from a major disability organization or university specifically for autistic, ADHD, or mixed-neurotype couples. Shallow should not fill that gap by asserting a new diagnostic relationship theory.

It can still record concrete design requirements supported across neurodivergent-affirming practice:

- do not equate eye contact, facial display, rapid verbal response, or spontaneous affection with care;
- allow important questions in advance and answers in writing;
- ask how each person detects overload, shutdown, pain, fatigue, and loss of language;
- negotiate pause-and-return procedures that do not punish processing time;
- map sensory needs for sound, light, touch, sleep, food, temperature, crowds, and sex;
- allocate planning, bills, appointments, cleaning, and social coordination according to actual capacity rather than moralizing executive-function differences;
- distinguish solitude used for regulation from punitive withdrawal;
- permit separate sleep arrangements and private spaces without treating them as marital failure;
- document agreements in accessible forms and review them as needs change;
- discuss masking, burnout, disclosure, work accommodations, and who may know a diagnosis;
- do not appoint the nondisabled or neurotypical partner as manager, interpreter, or permanent caregiver by default;
- preserve each partner's independent support network.

These should become ordinary Shallow questions available to any couple, not a stigmatizing side test.

## Chronic illness and care labor

Preparation should ask:

- What care is foreseeable now, and what care could emerge later?
- Which tasks can a spouse reasonably promise, and which require paid attendants, family, public benefits, or institutional support?
- Does either person fear being a burden, and has that fear prevented honest disclosure?
- Who controls medical information, appointments, medication, mobility devices, and emergency decisions?
- How will the couple prevent caregiving from becoming ownership or surveillance?
- What happens to paid work, retirement, insurance, and respite when care needs rise?
- Can either spouse refuse intimate care from the other without threatening the relationship?
- Is the home physically and sensorily usable by both people?
- What backup exists if the caregiving spouse becomes ill, leaves, or dies?

## Product implications for Shallow

1. Legal marriage, relational commitment, cohabitation, and caregiving arrangements must be represented separately.
2. The system must never infer that declining legal marriage means weak commitment.
3. Questions about care should be reciprocal but not falsely symmetrical; needs and capacities can differ substantially.
4. A person must be able to answer privately without a partner, parent, guardian, clergy member, or service provider controlling the response.
5. Accessibility is part of truthfulness. A rushed spoken answer may misrepresent someone who communicates more accurately in writing, with symbols, or after processing time.
6. Safety screening must distinguish support from control and consent from compliance.
7. Current benefit and parentage law should be retrieved by jurisdiction and date rather than stored as timeless advice.

## Remaining gaps

- A complete open neurodivergent premarital workbook produced by a credible disability-led institution.
- Deaf and DeafBlind couple preparation developed in sign language and visual formats.
- Marriage preparation for couples using personal-care attendants or supported living.
- Disability-led material on interabled household labor, sex, fertility, and parenting.
- Current state-by-state legal guides to marriage, guardianship, supported decision-making, benefits, and Medicaid home- and community-based services.
- Transnational LGBTQ parentage and marriage recognition.
