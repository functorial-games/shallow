# Hindu, Buddhist, and interfaith marriage-preparation resources

The strongest materials in this lane are not universal “Eastern” relationship guides. They are specific diaspora, institutional, ritual, and interfaith resources. Keep tradition, region, class, caste, migration history, and family structure visible rather than compressing them into cultural personality traits.

## Hindu and South Asian diaspora resources

### Hindu Spiritual Care Institute, “Getting Married”

- Publisher: Hindu Spiritual Care Institute.
- Link: https://hsciglobal.org/pages/getting-married/.
- Coverage: settlement and relocation, careers, children, languages, parenting, extended-family residence, holidays, community involvement, finances, inheritances, parental support, prenuptial agreements, wedding costs, food, religion in the home, rites of passage, crisis support, elder care, and interfaith marriage.
- Useful feature: makes visible the extended-family, migration, religious, and financial obligations hidden by couple-only workbooks. It asks specifically about long parental visits, support sent abroad, children's languages, household religious objects, and ceremonies.
- Caution: written for Hindus in the United States and sometimes speaks broadly about “Indian” or “Hindu” culture. Treat each prompt as a possible issue, not a fact about an individual or every family.
- Classification: **core practical and interfaith question source**.

### Interfaith Shaadi, *Critical Conversations Before Marriage*

- Publisher: Interfaith Shaadi.
- Landing page: https://interfaithshaadi.org/critical-conversations-before-marriage/.
- Coverage: extended families, money, faith, children, conflict, cultural identity, diaspora life, and counselor-led discussion.
- Useful feature: designed around interfaith and intercultural South Asian couples rather than adding religion as a final checkbox to a generic workbook.
- Caution: use the booklet's questions as prompts and independently check empirical or legal claims. The organization also publishes prescriptive religious-contract material that represents particular positions rather than all Hindus or interfaith couples.
- Classification: **interfaith question source with source screening**.

### Interfaith Shaadi, *American Hindu Prenuptial Agreement*

- Link: https://interfaithshaadi.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Hindu-Prenuptial-Agreement-25Aug2020.pdf.
- Coverage: financial disclosure, separate and shared property and debt, prior marriages and children, religious practice, conversion, children, funeral rites, monogamy, divorce, custody, and choice of civil law.
- Useful feature: demonstrates how legal, religious, and kin obligations can be written explicitly instead of left to assumptions.
- Caution: this is a model produced by an advocacy organization, not automatically enforceable legal advice and not representative of all Hindu traditions. Some clauses are strongly prescriptive and may be inequitable or legally ineffective. Each party would need independent counsel in the relevant jurisdiction.
- Classification: **contract research object, not a model to adopt wholesale**.

## Comparative multi-faith material

### Jain Academy, *Marriage: Reflections & Understandings from Nine Faiths in Britain*

- Publisher: Jain Academy with contributors from Bahá'í, Buddhist, Christian, Hindu, Muslim, Jain, Jewish, Sikh, and Zoroastrian communities.
- Open copy indexed at: https://docslib.org/doc/4151792/marrage-book-final.
- Coverage: wedding rites, beliefs, customs, family expectations, interfaith difficulties, and premarital guidance in a British multi-faith setting.
- Useful feature: places traditions side by side while explicitly warning that each contains internal diversity and that the chapters are introductions rather than final definitions.
- Caution: hosted copy is secondary and the work dates from 2010. Locate and preserve an authoritative publisher copy before treating it as a stable archive. Contributor accounts may present official or idealized practice rather than dissent or lived variation.
- Classification: **comparative orientation source**.

### Ismaili Muslim community, *Marital Harmony*

- Publisher: community material hosted by The Ismaili.
- Link: https://content-files.the.ismaili/files/Media/marital_harmony_cab.pdf.
- Coverage: finances and full disclosure, debt, assets, budgets, extended-family residence and support, interfaith marriage, faith practice, children, early childhood, and community resources.
- Useful feature: detailed treatment of extended family, privacy, money sent to relatives, interfaith life, and collective responsibilities.
- Caution: one Ismaili community resource, not a general account of Muslim or South Asian marriage; verify scientific claims in the child-development sections.
- Classification: **comparative community and financial question source**.

## Buddhist materials

### Foundation for the Preservation of the Mahayana Tradition, “A Buddhist Wedding Ceremony”

- Publisher: Foundation for the Preservation of the Mahayana Tradition.
- Link: https://fpmt.org/wp-content/uploads/teachers/zopa/advice/pdf/weddingceremonyeditedkm.pdf.
- Coverage: vows concerning compassion, patience, changing conditions, self-knowledge, conflict, service to others, impermanence, and eventual parting.
- Useful feature: offers a commitment text in which marriage is a practice ground rather than possession. It explicitly acknowledges difficulty, internal negativity, change, and the impossibility of holding anything forever.
- Caution: a Tibetan Buddhist-derived ceremony, not a premarital counseling curriculum and not representative of all Buddhist traditions. Its value is in the vows and conception of commitment.
- Classification: **comparative commitment text**.

### Vensi Anita Ria Gunawinata, “Module Development of Premarital Guidance for Buddhist Couples”

- Publication record: https://exa.ai/library/publication/g9bwn43x7ts.
- Context: module developed with a Theravada Buddhist setting in Surabaya, Indonesia.
- Coverage described in the paper: unrealistic expectations, Buddhist teachings, character and acceptance, communication, in-laws, marital roles, finances, and work-family conflict.
- Useful feature: begins from the proposition that romance alone is insufficient preparation and attempts to combine Dhamma with practical household adaptation.
- Caution: locate the original journal text and full module before extracting exercises. The study appears small and developmental; it does not establish long-term outcomes or transferability beyond its setting.
- Classification: **research lead, not yet a core resource**.

## Sikh and secular-humanist gaps

The search did not produce a complete, openly readable Sikh premarital counseling manual from a major gurdwara body or Sikh institution. It found descriptions of the *Anand Karaj* and wedding requirements, but ceremony guidance is not the same as preparation for the marriage.

The search also did not produce a strong, complete, openly licensed secular-humanist premarital curriculum from a major humanist organization. Commercial “secular workbooks” frequently repeat the same untraced divorce-prevention statistics and relationship-industry categories used in religious products.

These should remain explicit gaps rather than being filled with generic blog material.

## Interfaith questions Shallow should retain

- What does each person mean by participation in the other's ritual: respect, belief, family diplomacy, or unwanted conversion pressure?
- Which ceremonies, symbols, foods, clothing, calendars, and household objects will be present at home?
- What will children be told is true, not merely which holidays they will attend?
- Are rites such as baptism, circumcision, naming, initiation, dietary discipline, religious schooling, or funerals acceptable to both people?
- Does either family expect conversion before or after the wedding?
- Which partner bears more risk of estrangement, shame, violence, immigration consequences, or financial loss?
- How much money and care will go to parents and kin, including relatives abroad?
- Could parents live in the household, for how long, and with what authority?
- Which language or languages will the household and children use?
- What happens when one spouse becomes more observant, less observant, converts, or stops believing?
- Who has final authority over a child's religion when the parents cannot agree?
- Which legal system controls property, custody, divorce, and inheritance when religious documents or ceremonies conflict with civil law?
- Can each person state the other tradition's position accurately without caricature?

## Product implications

1. Shallow should store a person's own religious practices and obligations, not infer them from a label such as Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Muslim, Christian, Jewish, atheist, or “spiritual.”
2. Interfaith preparation should ask about acts, institutions, and future decisions rather than abstract “tolerance.”
3. Family influence should be mapped explicitly: emotional authority, housing, money, immigration sponsorship, care work, ritual authority, and the power to withdraw support.
4. A religious marriage contract and a civil marriage are separate objects with potentially conflicting rules.
5. The system should preserve unresolved disagreements rather than smoothing them into a synthetic shared spirituality neither person actually holds.
