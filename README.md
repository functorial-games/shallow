# Shallow

Working notes on love, desire, relationships, separation, conflict, and the ways these subjects are described in lived experience, literature, history, feminism, psychology, and popular advice.

## Start here

- [`lived-experience/`](lived-experience/) — learning from real events, relationship outcomes, and corrections of the assistant; public requirements, not private case histories
- [`yente/`](yente/) — the proposed matchmaker's experience-led learning and correction requirements
- [`history-of-love/`](history-of-love/) — poems, historical evidence, cross-cultural claims, and empirical work on love and attachment
- [`why-people-divorce/`](why-people-divorce/) — sources and questions about separation and divorce
- [`feminists/`](feminists/) — feminist writers and arguments relevant to the project
- [`books/`](books/) — provisional reading inventory and source notes

## Method

Keep primary evidence close. Separate description, theory, analogy, personal judgment, and empirical support. A useful source can still contain weak generalizations; a popular framework is not automatically a validated model.

Shallow and Yente must learn from the user's real experiences and failures, not only books. Preserve events and circumstances before imposing categories. Keep reported events, interpretations, assistant errors, corrections, and unknown motives distinct. A correction must change later responses, not merely prompt an apology.

This repository is public: private narratives and raw conversations do not belong in Git without separate, specific authorization. The new learning documents specify intended behavior; they do not implement persistent memory or an automatic learning system.

This repository is a research notebook, not relationship advice, diagnosis, or a settled scientific account of love.

## Research aim and additional source lanes

Shallow is an attempt to help people become intelligible to one another without making them write advertisements for themselves.

The project begins from evidence people have already produced through living: books kept, music heard repeatedly, films remembered, places returned to, activities practiced, objects saved, stories attached to those things, and the questions another person asks after seeing them.

It should not reduce a person to a personality type, compatibility percentage, list of slogans, or model-written dating persona.

## Current research areas

### [Books](books/README.md)

Personal inventory and research corpus for communication, personality classification, love, disclosure, conflict, separation, and ordinary life. Literary works remain scenes and arguments, not prompt mines or psychological authorities.

### [History of love](history-of-love/README.md)

Primary-source leads and critical notes on how love, desire, jealousy, attachment, separation, and erotic longing have been described across times and cultures.

### [Feminists](feminists/README.md)

A reading and research lane for women's writing about sex, desire, beauty, femininity, marriage, sexual difference, and the gap between ideology and lived experience, preserving disagreement rather than treating feminism as one position.

### [The exciting messaging phase](messaging/README.md)

What makes someone hope for a message from one particular person rather than merely check an app. The current design proposal is a private continuation notebook that remembers live conversational threads without writing counterfeit messages for the user.

### [Premarital counseling and marriage preparation](premarital-counseling/README.md)

Questions about what marriage promises, what binds when desire changes, what legitimately ends the obligation, and what duties survive separation. Includes an expanded [resource index](premarital-counseling/resources.md), a skeptical [evidence ledger](premarital-counseling/evidence.md), and separate maps for Orthodox and Black church, LGBTQ and disability-centered, Hindu, Buddhist, and interfaith materials.

### [Comparative history of marriage and divorce](history-of-marriage/README.md)

A separate archive on formation, divorce, remarriage, plural unions, property, labor, children, status, religion, state power, and the different meanings of marriage for elites, peasants, wage workers, enslaved people, and the propertyless. Includes an initial [comparative matrix](history-of-marriage/comparative-matrix.md), [annotated bibliography](history-of-marriage/bibliography.md), and a [recovery of the earlier discussion](history-of-marriage/recovered-prior-thread.md), including Hindu permanence, Japan, zero-property cases, child status after breakup, wife sale, and *Montaillou*.

## Research rules

1. Ask from evidence rather than demanding self-branding.
2. Preserve the difference between a person's record and the person's interpretation of it.
3. Treat models as aids to memory, comparison, retrieval, and question formation—not as hidden authors of intimate relationships.
4. Keep legal, economic, historical, literary, religious, and empirical claims distinguishable.
5. Report null results, adverse findings, source limits, and disagreement.
6. Do not treat continued marriage as success regardless of coercion, danger, or deprivation.
7. Do not import John Gottman or Gottman-associated claims as Shallow's scientific foundation.
8. Do not turn a disagreement into a compatibility score when the actual issue can be stated plainly.
