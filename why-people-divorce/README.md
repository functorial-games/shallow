# Why people divorce

Research for Shallow on **the difficult realities of long relationships and marriage**. The aim is to tell people truthfully what commitments may demand of them, not to make a spectacle of anyone's divorce.

**Product: aggregation only.** Shallow should provide patterns, rates, conditions, and representative *synthesized* language—not searchable case histories, personal memories, names, or original accusations. The research subject includes how people talk about their spouse; the released object is an analysis of how *groups* speak. The same applies to income, employment, residence and relationship histories.

## Questions

- At what point in a relationship do people begin reporting particular burdens, grievances, sacrifices or breakdowns?
- How does language shift from affection and reciprocity to scorekeeping, disillusionment, contempt, withdrawal, threats, repair or resignation?
- What happens following job loss, income change, moves, childrearing, caregiving, debt, illness or changes in sexual expectations?
- Which complaints are common in marriages that **continue**, not only marriages that end?
- Where do the two partners describe the same alleged event differently? How much role-blind agreement is possible before gender or other demographics are revealed?
- How do people describe a commitment they *intend to honor when it becomes unpleasant*? What would they tell someone preparing for marriage?

The central question for Shallow is not “will marriage make me happy?” but “what am I offering to do even when marriage is difficult?” Difficulty should not be confused with danger: violence, coercion, intimidation and threats are categorically different from ordinary disappointment and unequal domestic work.

## Work product

1. **Aggregate relationship timelines:** proportions, rates or distributions grouped by years together, married and separated, with clear denominators.
2. **Aggregate speech patterns:** coded themes (e.g. “I carried more than my share,” “I tried,” “you always,” “I never agreed to that”), disagreement rates and patterns of self-serving attribution—not identifying quotations.
3. **Contextual associations:** duration, housing moves *count*, broad labor-market/income changes, caregiving, children, and safety-relevant events, only where available from valid research datasets.
4. **Fair-warning questions for Shallow:** grounded questions about obligation, sacrifice, repair, boundaries, safety and resilience. Show sample limitations; avoid deterministic predictions about an individual.
5. **Role-blind and role-aware analysis:** start with anonymous Party A/B coding; optionally examine sex/gender and other categories at a sufficiently aggregated level to test rather than presume differences.

Release a statistic only with its population/cohort, observation window, numerator and denominator, source, missing-data limitations and uncertainty. Reports of *why* someone divorced are accounts, not necessarily causes, and an allegation is not a proven fact.

## Non-retention and privacy

- **Do not keep raw court records, case identifiers, interview transcripts, personal stories or individualized research records in this repository or in Shallow's public serving corpus.** Do not publish excerpts that can be searched back to a litigant.
- Favor existing research with consent, established confidential surveys and already-published statistical tables. If analysis of legitimately obtained text becomes useful, treat it as a transient and access-controlled computation, subject to legal/ethical review: extract only codes and counts, audit disclosure risk, discard source copies, and release aggregates only.
- Removing names or genders does **not** make detailed records anonymous. A combination of ZIP histories, incomes, dates, children and rare incidents may re-identify people. Avoid granular geography and tiny cross-tabs; require privacy review even for aggregates.
- For representative dialogue, use *invented composites explicitly labeled synthetic*. Never present invented sentences as testimony.
- Public court access does not authorize bulk scraping or republication. In particular, MiCOURT prohibits bulk downloads.
- Do not use remaining married as the only definition of success. It is not Shallow's task to persuade someone to stay in an unsafe relationship.
